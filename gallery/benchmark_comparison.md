## 2. Eight-Dimension Comparison Against Published *Nature* & *Science* Figures

### 1. Physical Column Width Fidelity
* **Published benchmark:** *Nature* typesets main figures strictly to **89 mm** (single column), **120 mm** (1.5 column), or **183 mm** (double column), max height 170 mm (247 mm full page). *Science* uses **55 mm** (1-col), **120 mm** (2-col), and **184 mm** (3-col full width). Submitting off-size figures forces journal production teams to rescale the entire PDF, which distorts stroke weights and drives text below minimum legibility.
* **Default Matplotlib failure:** Defaults to `6.4 × 4.8 in` (`162.56 × 121.92 mm`), and common `bbox_inches="tight"` habits silently mutate figure dimensions depending on tick-label width.
* **`figmint` result:** `Plate` locks exact millimeter canvas geometry at construction and `save_figure(..., lock_dimensions=True)` disables `bbox_inches="tight"`, yielding **0.00 mm MediaBox deviation** across every generated PDF (`89.00 mm`, `183.00 mm`, `184.00 mm`).

### 2. Typography & Math Font Consistency
* **Published benchmark:** Both *Nature* and *Science* require clean sans-serif typography (Helvetica or Arial) consistently across axis titles, tick labels, units, and mathematical symbols: **5–7 pt** (body ~6.5 pt, ticks ~6 pt) for *Nature*, and **6–8 pt** (body ~7 pt) for *Science*. Mixing serif LaTeX/Computer Modern math (`Cmr10`/`Cmmi10`) inside sans-serif axis labels is one of the most frequent copyediting flags.
* **Default Matplotlib failure:** Uses DejaVu Sans at 10–12 pt paired with serif Computer Modern math (`Cmr10`, `Cmmi10` detected in our Before PDF), producing mismatched x-heights and serifs on symbols like $\mathrm{IC}_{50}$ and $\mu\mathrm{M}$.
* **`figmint` result:** Automatically resolves installed `Helvetica` (falling back gracefully to Arial / Liberation Sans / DejaVu Sans) and binds custom sans-serif mathtext so $\mathrm{IC}_{50}$, $\mu\mathrm{M}$, $\Delta T$, $\mathrm{CO}_2$, $\mathrm{CH}_4$, and $\mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$ match surrounding body copy identically—at true 1:1 print point sizes (`5.0–8.0 pt` in `example2_nature_plate.pdf`).

### 3. Panel Label Alignment & Casing
* **Published benchmark:** A hallmark of *Nature* vs. *Science* house style is panel labeling: *Nature* requires **lowercase bold** (`a, b, c, d`, 8 pt) while *Science* requires **uppercase bold** (`A, B, C`, 8.5 pt). In published plates, labels in the same column share a vertical left rail and labels in the same row share a horizontal baseline, sitting cleanly outside y-axis titles without colliding with data or tick marks.
* **`figmint` result:** `Plate.label_panels()` draws the canvas, measures each panel's rendered tight bounding box (including y-tick labels and axis titles), reserves safe top/left margin in `constrained_layout`, and aligns labels across shared columns and rows automatically. Switching `journal="nature"` to `journal="science"` in `example3` automatically flips `a, b, c` (8.0 pt) to `A, B, C` (8.5 pt) while keeping every column and row aligned.

### 4. Ink-to-Data Ratio & Whitespace
* **Published benchmark:** Top-tier journal figures strip chartjunk—removing top/right bounding spines, background grids, and boxed legends that obscure curves—while using restrained hairline-safe strokes (`0.5 pt` axes, `0.9–1.1 pt` data traces), outward ticks, direct series labeling, and clean statistical brackets.
* **`figmint` result:**
  * In `before_after_nature.png`, removing the boxed legend, gridlines, and top/right spines while adding 95% CI ribbons, trimmed data-range bottom spine, and auto-separated `direct_labels` eliminates occlusion of the lower dose-response curves.
  * In `example2_nature_plate.png`, `sig_bracket` auto-stacks three p-value comparisons (`p = 0.0018`, `p = 0.0003`, `p < 0.0001`) without collision, `zoom_inset` magnifies the `0–3 h` STAT3 activation peak cleanly inside open whitespace, and `preflight_figure` verifies zero text collisions across all 64 visible text artists.

### 5. Colorblind & Grayscale Robustness
* **Published benchmark:** *Nature* explicitly advises against rainbow/`jet` colormaps and red-green-only encodings, recommending perceptually uniform colormaps (`viridis`, `magma`, `cividis`) and colorblind-safe categorical palettes that remain distinguishable in monochrome print.
* **`figmint` result:**
  * `preflight_figure` rejects banned rainbow colormaps (`jet`, `rainbow`, `hsv`, etc.); Panel `a` of `example2_nature_plate.png` uses perceptual `magma`.
  * `plate.save_proof("gallery/output/example2_nature_proof.png")` renders linear-sRGB Machado 2009 **Deuteranopia** and **Protanopia** simulations alongside a **Rec. 709 Grayscale** luminance preview directly beneath the full-color plate. During visual inspection of our initial proof sheet, the grayscale check immediately revealed that light peach (`#F39B7F`) had weak contrast for the `Quiescent` UMAP cluster; swapping to NPG warm umber (`#7E6148`) restored strong luminance separation across all three CVD/print views.

### 6. Hybrid Vector / Raster PDF Size
* **Published benchmark:** Journals enforce upload limits (typically ≤10 MB per figure) and editorial vector workflows freeze or crash in Illustrator when single-cell UMAPs or genome-wide scatter plots embed 25,000–100,000 vector circles.
* **`figmint` result:** `save_figure(..., rasterize_above=10_000)` selectively rasterizes dense data collections (`PathCollection`, `QuadMesh`, etc.) at journal combo DPI (`500 DPI` for *Nature*, `600 DPI` for *Science*) while keeping every axis spine, tick, colorbar label, scale bar, and cluster text as crisp vector geometry:
  * Pure vector 3-panel climate plates (`example3_nature.pdf`, `example3_science.pdf`): **`34.3 KB`** and **`34.1 KB`**.
  * 4-panel multi-modal biology plate with a `310 × 232` confocal micrograph **plus** a **25,000-point** UMAP scatter (`example2_nature_plate.pdf`): **`985.5 KB` (`0.96 MB`)**—well under 1 MB while remaining razor-sharp at print magnification.

### 7. Adobe Illustrator & Production Editability (Type 42 vs. Type 3)
* **Published benchmark:** *Nature* and *Science* artwork checkers require live editable vector text (TrueType / CID Type 0 / Type 42 in PDF, un-outlined text in SVG) so production editors can adjust copy or apply house fonts without re-typesetting entire panels. Matplotlib's default `pdf.fonttype = 3` outlines glyphs into uneditable Type 3 bitmaps/paths.
* **`figmint` result:** Enforces `pdf.fonttype = 42`, `ps.fonttype = 42`, and `svg.fonttype = "none"`. `pypdf` inspection confirms **0 `/Type3` fonts** across all four `figmint` PDFs (every font is embedded as editable `/Type0` TrueType `Helvetica` / `Helvetica-Bold`), whereas the stock Matplotlib Before PDF contained **4 `/Type3` fonts**.

### 8. Honest Limitations: What Still Benefits from Manual Illustration
`figmint` closes ~90% of the gap between raw Python plotting scripts and print-ready *Nature* / *Science* quantitative data plates, but it is **not** a replacement for vector illustration tools where visual storytelling goes beyond statistical axes:
1. **Biological & experimental schematics:** Anatomical diagrams, signaling pathway cartoons, experimental timelines with mice/cells/microfluidic chips, and graphical abstracts are still authored far more effectively in Adobe Illustrator, Inkscape, or BioRender.
2. **3D molecular & structural renders:** Cryo-EM density maps, protein ribbons, and 3D materials meshes come from PyMOL, ChimeraX, or Blender and should be composited as high-DPI raster panels (300–600 DPI) alongside `figmint` data panels.
3. **Bespoke microscopy overlays & irregular callouts:** Freeform ROI outlines, curved leader arrows around irregular tissue structures, and multi-channel LUT composites often benefit from ImageJ/Fiji + final Illustrator polish.
4. **Final journal copyediting:** Journal art departments occasionally tweak kerning, break lines in multi-line labels, or nudge panel margins to fit tight page budgets. Because `figmint` exports exact-mm Type 42 PDFs (and `svg.fonttype="none"` SVGs) with clean layer hierarchy and zero Type 3 outlines, opening a `figmint` PDF in Adobe Illustrator for that final 5% polish is frictionless.
